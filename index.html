Abstract

Physical caregiving robots hold promise for improving the quality of life of millions who require assistance with Activities of Daily Living. They must operate in people’s homes over the long term, yet most in-home studies are short-term, and longer deployments of physically assistive robots have been either teleoperated or autonomous with fixed behavior, so only the person could adapt.

In this work, we deploy a mobile manipulator for a month in the home of a care recipient with Multiple Sclerosis and show human–robot co-adaptation: the robot adapts to her and she to it. The robot can provide mealtime assistance autonomously, from retrieving the meal from the refrigerator and heating it in the microwave to feeding, drinking, and mouth wiping to clearing the plate. It can do so with no input from her, or take her input at various levels, from a preference correction up to full teleoperation. She eats with it alone, with the TV on, with music, or games on her iPad, on video calls with family, and with a friend.

Our key insight is that preferences are correlated, so one correction informs others; a large language model predicts her preferences jointly from her history and, after each correction, re-predicts those she has not yet confirmed, requiring 27% fewer corrections in retrospective evaluation than the strongest baseline. Her corrections fall to near zero by the second week, resurface as she becomes more comfortable with the robot and starts exploring new behaviors and her preferences change, and fall again as the robot adapts through this non-stationarity.

She adapts as well: when talking at social meals confuses the robot’s open-mouth detector, she switches to a button, and by the final day learns to talk with her mouth slightly open, opening it wide only for a bite. That day, over a video call, one correction is all the meal needs; the robot revises five other predictions from it, all matching hers. Over the month, her reported workload falls and her trust in the robot rises to the top of the scale.
